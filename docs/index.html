Aim Short to Reach Far: Your Frozen World Model Can Plan Better Than You Think

World-model planners usually aim straight at the goal. Aiming at a nearby point on a remembered route lets the same frozen model reach goals it otherwise misses, with no training.

Why aiming at the goal fails

Reaching a goal can require first moving away from it. When every candidate action is scored by its distance to the goal, the first steps of a good route look worse than standing still, so the planner rejects them, even if the world model predicts every outcome exactly. A nearby target taken from a recorded route rewards those first steps instead.

How Anchored Planning aims short

  1. Find a recorded moment that looks like now and later reached something that looks like the goal.
  2. Take the observation five steps after that moment as the target.
  3. Let the frozen world model predict where each candidate action leads, pick the one that lands closest to the target, execute a few steps, and repeat.

The recorded route decides where to aim; the world model decides how to get there. Nothing is trained.

The farther the goal, the bigger the gap

As the goal moves from 25 to 100 actions away, the average gain from aiming short grows from 33.4 to 47.9 points.

More search does not close the gap

What this means

Planning success with goal distance is how world models are usually compared. Our results show that this number reflects the planning objective, not just the model.
