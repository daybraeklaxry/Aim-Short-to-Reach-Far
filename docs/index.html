01 / Abstract

Change the target.
Keep the model.

Visual world models predict action outcomes, while planners commonly score those outcomes by their distance to the encoded goal image. We show that this target can limit control even with exact dynamics and globally optimal short-horizon search: reaching a goal may require actions that initially move away from it. With frozen LeWM models, intermediate targets substantially improve action synthesis and recorded-action ranking on Cube, PushT, Reacher, and TwoRoom. Learned targets and targets drawn from observed experience both produce these gains. We introduce Anchored Planning, which retrieves a recorded segment whose start and end resemble the current and goal observations, then aims at an observation shortly after its start. The frozen model scores actions toward this target from the current state. Without additional training, observed targets outperform the released LeWM on every task in our long-range evaluation. Additional final-goal search falls short of the same gains. Target placement and retrieval timing further shape success, while lower successor-prediction error need not translate into better control. Final-goal scoring can therefore understate what a frozen world model supports, and target choice should be assessed alongside prediction accuracy.

04 / Why it works

The shortest-looking step
can miss the route.

Reaching a goal may require first moving away from it. A short-horizon planner that minimizes final-goal distance can reject exactly those actions.

Our counterexample makes this distinction explicit: even with exact dynamics and globally optimal short-horizon search, final-goal scoring can stall. Successive observed targets along the same feasible route enable progress.

Experience tells us where to aim.
Prediction tells us how to approach it.

A recorded segment connects a nearby observation to a distant outcome. Anchored Planning retrieves that segment and uses its short-term observed successor as the planning target.

  1. Retrieve a routeMatch the current image to a recorded start, and the goal image to the segment’s distant endpoint.
  2. Aim at an observationUse the observation five actions after the retrieved start as the default target.
  3. Predict from hereThe frozen world model evaluates actions from the live state. The same target supports continuous action synthesis and selection among recorded actions.
