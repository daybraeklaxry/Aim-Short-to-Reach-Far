Aim Short to Reach Far: Your Frozen World Model Can Plan Better Than You Think

Change only the planning target, and the same frozen world model and planner reach goals that goal-distance planning misses.

Xvyuan Liu, Jianjie Fang, Chen Gao, Yong Li

Tsinghua University

Correspondence: chgao96@gmail.com liyong07@tsinghua.edu.cn

9.2% → 59.8%
Mean CEM success across four tasks at standard starts, changing only the target.
67.0% → 83.8%
Mean success when ranking the same eight retrieved action blocks.
2 > 30
Intermediate-target iterations beat thirty final-goal iterations on every task.

The closest step is not always on the route

World-model planners usually pick the action whose predicted outcome lands closest to the goal image. But reaching a goal can require first moving away from it. Short-horizon distance scoring rejects those first steps, even when the model predicts them perfectly. In a smooth system with exact predictions and globally optimal search, goal-distance planning stays still forever, while six intermediate targets along a recorded route reach the goal.

The constructed example is given in Proposition 1; Appendix B contains the proof.

Aim at where experience went next

Anchored Planning retrieves a recorded segment whose start looks like the current observation and whose end looks like the goal, then aims at the observation five actions after that start. The frozen world model predicts where candidate actions lead from the current state and picks the one closest to this nearby target. As execution proceeds, retrieval uses shorter recorded spans, so the target keeps moving along a route toward the goal. No model is trained.

AP-CEM synthesizes actions. AP-rank selects among recorded action blocks. Both use observed targets.

The gap grows with distance

From the shortest offset to 100 actions, the mean gain from observed targets grows from 33.4 to 47.9 points for CEM.

Goal offset is the number of recorded actions between the start and goal images. The planner’s lookahead stays fixed.

More search does not close the gap

Two intermediate-target iterations beat thirty final-goal iterations on every task.

What this means for world models

Goal-distance planning success is a common benchmark for world models. It reflects the planning objective as well as the predictor.
